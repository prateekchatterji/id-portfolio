# Unit Specifications

All practice items in U0 to U2 are newly written so each one isolates a single gap. U3 can draw on an existing item bank tagged by question type. Times and item counts are planning figures, to be adjusted after U0 to U2 actuals. **[Judgement]**

## U0 Entry probe (screen)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Objective** | Screen whether the gap is specific to numeric arguments; set baselines for non-regression |
| **Items** | 12 new, mixed, unlabelled, 2:00 each: 3 count-vs-rate, 3 basis-of-comparison, 4 non-numeric causal, 2 structure |
| **Time** | 24 min |
| **Measures** | Numeric (6) vs non-numeric (4) accuracy; structure (2) as baseline |
| **Pass rule** | Numeric at most 3 of 6 and non-numeric at least 3 of 4: run the full pathway |
| **If not** | Both low: foundation review first. Numeric at least 5 of 6: skip to U3 |

## U1 Concepts: the two checks and the trigger

| | |
|---|---|
| **Objective** | **Check 1 (count vs rate):** does the evidence's metric match the conclusion's? If not, what denominator moved? **Check 2 (basis of comparison):** which groups, grouped on which side (cause, effect or time), measured alike? **Trigger:** any number in the argument means running both checks before reading the options |
| **Activities** | Worked examples built from the learner's own Q3, Q4 and Q5. Reframe: "a true premise can still fail to support the conclusion." Cue: find the phrase that names the basis ("on the basis of this comparison"). Write a tag (DENOM, COMPARE, CAUSE) before the options; no answer before 1:30 |
| **Checkpoint** | New stems with no options: label each stem's metric and basis. 15 count-vs-rate, 12 comparison |
| **Time** | 100 min |
| **Pass rule** | Check 1 at least 14 of 15; Check 2 at least 11 of 12 |
| **If not** | First miss: new worked examples and fresh stems for that check. Second miss: escalate to a full concept review, then retest |

## U2 Labelled application and contrast pairs

| | |
|---|---|
| **Objective** | Apply both checks across assumption, weaken, strengthen and evaluate questions; prove the method is chosen from the metric, not from habit |
| **Part A** | 12 count-vs-rate and 12 comparison questions, medium difficulty, labelled "numeric argument"; full written pre-think and tag |
| **Part B** | 6 count-vs-rate pairs; 6 measured-alike vs not pairs; 8 valid-comparison items where the answer lies elsewhere |
| **Time** | 105 min |
| **Pass rule** | Per gap at least 80%; pre-think present at least 90%; tag matches the key's category at least 80%; pairs at least 5 of 6 per gap; false alarms at most 1 of 8 |
| **If not** | Part A gap fails: back to that check in U1. Pairs fail: fresh pairs of that type. False alarms: more valid-comparison items |

## U3 Unlabelled, mixed, timed, with fade

| | |
|---|---|
| **Objective** | Deploy the checks without labels at test pace; fade the written pre-think; confirm strengths held |
| **Items** | 4 sets of 13 mixed questions, 5 to 6 numeric per set (20+ numeric), 2:00 each. Set 1: one-word tag. Sets 2 to 4: nothing written |
| **Time** | 4 × 30 min |
| **Pass rule** | Numeric at least 70% (last 20 numeric); at most 10% answered under 1:30; average numeric time at most 2:15; set-1 tag match at least 80%; unwritten within 5 points of written; non-numeric no drop vs U0 |
| **If not** | See the branching rules in `02-design/adaptive-pathway.md` |

## U4 Consolidation and retention (exit)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Objective** | Show the fix holds under mixed, timed conditions, matters at score-relevant difficulty, and lasts |
| **Activities** | Mixed medium quizzes, then hard once medium is passed. Log every miss by tag. Track the rolling 20-question hard benchmark after two hard quizzes. Retention: two hard quizzes after about two weeks on other topics |
| **Pass rule** | See `05-evaluate/metrics-and-exit.md` |

## Authoring load

About 95 new items for U0 to U2 (12 + 15 + 12 + 24 + 32), plus about 52 for U3 unless tagged bank items are used. Units were capped at five by merging; the remaining cost is items, not units.

## Sample items

**A contrast pair (Gap 1).** Same story; one detail changes the right answer.

> **Half 1.** A clinic's share of patients who missed follow-up appointments fell from 20 percent to 12 percent after it began sending text reminders. The clinic concludes the reminders reduced missed appointments.
> *The number of patients the clinic sees has doubled.* Does this weaken the conclusion? **No.** The evidence is already a share, so patient volume doesn't change it.
>
> **Half 2.** A clinic's number of patients who missed follow-up appointments fell from 200 to 120 after it began sending text reminders. The clinic concludes the reminders made patients less likely to miss appointments.
> *The number of patients the clinic sees has halved.* Does this weaken the conclusion? **Yes.** The evidence is a count; with half the patients, 120 misses is a higher rate than before.

A learner who attacks every number fails Half 1; a learner who never asks about the denominator fails Half 2. Only the right check passes both.

**A valid-comparison item (guards over-correction).**

> Two warehouses use the same scanners and the same audit procedure. Last quarter, Warehouse A's audited picking error rate was 1.1 percent and Warehouse B's was 2.4 percent. Warehouse B's manager concludes that B has a picking-accuracy problem worth investigating.

The comparison is sound: same measure, same procedure, both rates. A learner who doubts this one is over-correcting.
