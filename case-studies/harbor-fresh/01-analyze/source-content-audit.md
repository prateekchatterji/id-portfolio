# Source Content Audit

**Source:** the author's recorded walkthrough of one argument-analysis question (24 min 26 s, slides plus narration).
**Not reproduced here:** the source uses a third-party test item. It has been replaced by the original *Harbor Fresh* case.

## Segment audit

| Time | Segment | Verdict | Becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:54 | Hook ("you probably picked a helpful choice") and overview | Keep hook; cut preamble | Opening scenario: learner chooses first, then sees why |
| 1:54–4:09 | Three parts: question, argument, options | Keep | Labeled graphic |
| 4:09–6:47 | Summarize the argument as a shorthand chain | Keep; core method | Build-the-chain activity |
| 6:50–~11:00 | Pass 1: sort options into strengthener or irrelevant | Keep; make interactive | Sorting activity |
| ~11:00–21:00 | Pass 2: negation test; A vs. D | Keep; the most distinctive idea | "Race the negations" scenario |
| 21:00–23:20 | Takeaway notes and practice task | Keep | One-page job aid |
| 23:20–24:26 | Reflection questions and error log | Keep | End-of-module reflection and error-log template |

## What changes and why

| Source | Redesign | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 24 minutes, one continuous video | 15–20 minutes in short sections | 20-minute learning windows |
| 12 minutes of option analysis with no learner action | Learner acts every 2–3 minutes | Active practice beats passive viewing |
| Test-prep context | Business memo context | Match the audience's actual work |
| Practice set as homework | Practice with feedback inside the module | Feedback while the error is fresh |
| Dense text slides | Chunked screens and visuals | Lower cognitive load for second-language readers |

## What stays exactly
The logic chain ("every arrow is a leap someone can attack"), the two-pass method, the negation test, "race the negations" and the escape route, and "helpful is not essential."
