# Assessment Item: *Harbor Fresh*

## Argument
Harbor Fresh, a grocery chain, required every store manager in its Eastern region to complete a new demand-forecasting course by the end of March. Each store manager places the store's produce orders weekly. In the two quarters that followed, Eastern stores discarded 22 percent less fresh produce than in the same two quarters a year earlier. The regional director concludes that the course, by teaching managers to forecast customer demand more accurately, caused the reduction in spoilage.

## Question
Which of the following are assumptions on which the regional director's argument depends? *Select all that apply.*

- (A) Eastern stores did not install new refrigerated display cases during those two quarters.
- (B) Most managers rated the course highly on the end-of-course survey.
- (C) The lower spoilage improved the Eastern region's profit margins.
- (D) Harbor Fresh's central replenishment system did not override the produce order quantities that Eastern managers entered.
- (E) Fresh produce is the only product category for which the forecasting method is useful.
- (F) Eastern managers were using the course's forecasting method for the weekly produce orders placed during the two quarters in which spoilage fell.
- (G) A competing grocery chain in the same region, which offered no such course, saw its spoilage stay level over the same period.
- (H) Orders placed using the method matched customer demand more closely than orders placed before the course.

## The logic chain
Course → managers learn forecasting → managers use it for weekly orders → orders reach the shelf unchanged → orders match demand → less produce discarded

## Answer: D, F and H

| Option | Role | Negated, the argument… |
|---|---|---|
| **D** | Assumption: guards "orders reach the shelf unchanged" | …breaks. The system replaced managers' orders, so better forecasting never affected stock. No escape route. |
| **F** | Assumption: guards "managers use it" during the measured period | …breaks. The method was not in use while spoilage fell, so it cannot be the cause. No escape route. |
| **H** | Assumption: guards "orders match demand" | …breaks. The forecasts were no better, so the mechanism fails. No escape route. |
| A | Near miss: strengthener | …is weakened, but survives. The course and new cases could both have helped. Escape route. |
| G | Trap: different population | …is unaffected. A competitor's spoilage says nothing decisive about Harbor Fresh. |
| B | Irrelevant | A Level 1 reaction score says nothing about the Level 4 result. |
| C | Beyond the conclusion | The conclusion is about spoilage, not profit. |
| E | Irrelevant | Other product categories are outside the claim. |

**Race the negations (A vs. D):** both look necessary. A-negated leaves an escape route (a second cause alongside the course); D-negated leaves none.
