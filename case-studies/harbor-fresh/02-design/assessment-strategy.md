# Assessment Strategy

## 1. Purpose
Show that learners can apply the method to arguments they have **not** seen. Harbor Fresh is the worked example and Meridian Bank is the practice case, so the final assessment uses two new cases.

## 2. Assessment types

| Type | Where | Scored? | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-check | S02 (Harbor Fresh) | No | Activation; shows learners the gap in their current approach |
| Formative | S04–S10 | No (feedback only) | Practice with immediate explanation |
| Summative | Final quiz lesson | Yes, pass at 80% | Evidence of the terminal objective |
| On the job | Manager spot-check of memos at 60 days | Outside the module | Level 3 behavior (see the project brief) |

## 3. Final assessment blueprint (6 items, about 4 minutes)

Two new cases, three items each:

| Item | Case | Format | Objectives | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Case X | Multiple response | EO2, EO3, EO5 | Find all required assumptions; traps as distractors |
| 2 | Case X | Multiple choice | EO4, EO5 | Pick the statement that only strengthens (the near miss) |
| 3 | Case X | Multiple choice | EO6 | Pick the question that best tests the weakest link |
| 4 | Case Y | Multiple response | EO2, EO3, EO5 | As item 1, on a new argument |
| 5 | Case Y | Multiple choice | EO4, EO5 | As item 2 |
| 6 | Case Y | Multiple choice | EO6 | As item 3 |

**Pass:** 5 of 6 (83%), which meets the 80% target. Unlimited retakes; items shuffle on retake.

**Scoring note:** Rise scores a multiple-response item as fully right or fully wrong. That is intended: finding some but not all of the required assumptions still leaves a memo exposed.

The two new cases are written in the Develop phase, to the item-writing rules below, and checked in the Round 2 pilot.

## 4. Item-writing rules
1. **Original content only.** No items adapted from published tests.
2. **One conclusion per argument**, stated in the argument's last sentence.
3. **Each key guards exactly one link** of the chain, and has no escape route when negated.
4. **Each distractor has a named role:** near miss, different population, reaction data, beyond the conclusion, or irrelevant. A distractor with no role is cut.
5. **The number of keys varies** (Harbor Fresh has three; Meridian Bank has two) so learners can't guess from a pattern.
6. **Every item is negation-tested by a second reviewer** before release. The Option F revision (R-01) shows why.
7. **Plain wording:** under 25 words per option; no double negatives in the stem.

## 5. Monitoring after launch
Results are reviewed each quarter and compared with the agreed targets:

| Measure | Flag when | Action |
|---|---|---|
| First-attempt pass rate | Below 70% | Review the items and the teaching screens they test |
| A key's selection rate | Below 50% | Check wording; check that the practice covers that link type |
| A distractor's selection rate | Above 50% | Re-run the negation test; the distractor may be defensible |
| Retakes needed to pass | Median above 2 | Review feedback quality |

Findings and changes go into `05-evaluate/revision-log.md`, as in the pilot.
