# Conceptual Design: *Mind the Gap*

## 1. Design summary

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| Audience | Analysts, 0–2 years, global, English as a second language for many |
| Goal | Find and test the hidden assumptions in a business recommendation |
| Modality | Self-paced web-based module in Articulate Rise, plus a one-page job aid |
| Length | About 20 minutes: 15–16 min of lessons, 4 min final assessment |
| Core case | *Harbor Fresh* (worked example), then *Meridian Bank* (practice) |
| Pass mark | 80% on the final assessment |
| Delivery | SCORM package for the client LMS; web export for this portfolio |

## 2. Instructional strategy

The design follows **Merrill's First Principles of Instruction**: learning is anchored in a real problem, activates what learners already do, demonstrates the method, has them apply it, and helps them carry it into their work.

| Principle | How the module does it |
|---|---|
| Problem-centered | Every screen works on a real-looking business recommendation, not abstract logic |
| Activation | Learners try the Harbor Fresh question *before* any teaching (S02). Most will pick a "helpful" option, which makes the lesson that follows matter. |
| Demonstration | A worked example: the Harbor Fresh argument is taken apart step by step (S03–S07) |
| Application | Practice on a new case with less support (S09), then a stakeholder question (S10) |
| Integration | Job aid for live work; reflection prompts for an error log (S11) |

**Supporting choices**
- **Worked example, then faded practice.** Novices learn a method faster by studying a full solution first. Support is then withdrawn: Harbor Fresh is fully worked; Meridian Bank gives prompts; the final assessment gives none.
- **The two-pass method.** Pass 1 sorts out what is plainly outside the argument; pass 2 tests the rest by negation. Learners stop forcing a verdict too early.
- **Write out every link (from Round 1, R-06).** Testers without prior practice found the obvious link and missed the hidden ones. The chain step (S04) makes learners predict each link, including links the text never states, before they see the options.
- **Name the traps (from Round 1, R-07).** A callout after the sort (S05) addresses reaction data offered as proof of impact ("liking a course isn't using it").
- **Low cognitive load.** One idea per screen; the argument stays visible whenever learners judge options; plain, short sentences for second-language readers.

## 3. Structure and timing

| Section | Screens | Minutes | Main Rise blocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start | S01 Welcome; S02 Try it first | 3 | Statement, list; multiple-response knowledge check |
| Take the argument apart | S03 Three parts; S04 Build the chain | 2.5 | Labeled graphic; process |
| Test the options | S05 Pass 1 sort; S06 Negation test; S07 Race the negations; S08 Check your first answer | 5.5 | Sorting activity; flashcards; scenario; table |
| Practice | S09 Meridian Bank; S10 Your stakeholder question | 3.5 | Multiple-response and multiple-choice knowledge checks |
| Close | S11 Takeaways and job aid | 1 | List; attachment |
| Final assessment | 6 items, 2 new cases | 4 | Rise quiz lesson |

**Interaction rule:** the learner acts at least every 2–3 minutes. The source recording had a 12-minute stretch with no learner action; this design has none longer than 2 minutes.

## 4. Why Rise
- **Responsive by default.** Round 1 testers used a laptop, phone and tablet; Rise adapts to all three without extra work.
- **The needed interactions are built in:** sorting activity, process, flashcards, scenario and knowledge checks.
- **One-click SCORM export** for the client LMS, plus a web export for hosting.
- **Fast to update** after the pilot.

Captivate is kept for what it does best: a short software simulation of filling in the error-log template (v1.3).

## 5. Feedback strategy
- Feedback explains *why*, using the negation test: "Negate it: … The argument still survives, because …"
- Wrong answers are named by type (near miss, different population, beyond the conclusion, reaction data) so learners build a vocabulary for their errors.
- Tone: direct and encouraging, never "Wrong!"
- Where a Rise block cannot show feedback for each option separately, the explanation appears on the following screen.

## 6. Visual and tone direction
- Clean, text-light screens, one accent color, generous white space.
- The chain is the recurring visual: a row of links, each labeled, highlighted when tested.
- Scenario characters (S07) are diverse and named neutrally (e.g., Asha, an engagement manager).
- Voice: a senior colleague explaining over coffee: plain words, short sentences, no jargon without a definition.

## 7. Accessibility and localization
As set out in the needs analysis: idiom-free text (the title is glossed on S01), captions for any audio, keyboard-operable interactions, contrast that meets WCAG AA, meaning never carried by color alone, and text kept out of images so the module can be translated later.

## 8. Out of scope
Audio narration (added only if the sponsor requests it; this doubles the time needed for updates), translation, and LMS reporting configuration.
